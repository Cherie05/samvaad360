## Remaining work in order

Customer relationship rollout also needs a privately hosted HTTPS API, configured staff/customer identities, an approved actual LMS/CRM mapping and separate private Snowflake writer activation. The manual CSV/JSON import path and local customer forms are implemented; continuous source ingestion and production customer authentication are not. The [relationship plan](docs/customer-relationship-plan.md#production-rollout-gates) gives the concrete activation and acceptance sequence.

1. Activate the implemented controlled Twilio pilot: privately configure the provider account, authorised caller number, Verify service, publicly reachable HTTPS API, persistent pilot database and staff OIDC/token bridge. Use exact authorised test destinations, genuine possession verification and an approved operational action. Verify an answered call, signed callbacks, opt-out, cancellation and manual reconciliation before claiming delivery. See [the telephone runbook](docs/real-telephony-pilot.md). Stock provider speech supports this path; a neural model, PBX and SIP trunk are not prerequisites for it.
2. Implement production staff and borrower identity, tenant/customer authorization and a PostgreSQL operational repository/outbox. Preserve atomic approvals, consent, action claims and event deduplication; add durable workers, leases/watchdogs, backup/restore, retention, alerts and shared quotas. The pilot already holds uncertain dispatches and permits manual provider reconciliation, but has no automatic recovery worker. Standard Snowflake keys are unenforced; hybrid tables are unavailable in standard trials.
3. Complete the time-sensitive hackathon submission in parallel: verify actual CoCo account/skill/hook invocation, record the required 3–5 minute workflow video demonstrating 2–3 capabilities, adapt the completed custom PDF to the organizer template under 5 MB and supply a brief under 1024 characters. Public GitHub and website links are verified; a Cortex `OK` does not establish CoCo execution. Separately confirm the private review's exact status after manual refresh, evaluate grounded Cortex answers and check current credits/expiry.
4. If choosing the optional self-hosted voice path, pin and review the neural TTS/tokenizer/codec/runtime bundle, benchmark phone-bandwidth speech and English/Hindi recognition, implement Asterisk ARI/media workers and contract a licensed SIP trunk. Parler Mini v1 remains provisional; v1.1 is blocked pending tokenizer terms. This path has its own licence, asset, carrier and live-call acceptance gates.
5. Complete institutional production acceptance for the chosen telephone path: strong borrower authentication before financial disclosure, measured speech quality and latency, staffed human transfer, monitored crash recovery and narrowly authorised lender integrations. A permitted pilot call alone does not establish production readiness or a financial workflow.

The local Windows voice uses installed OS features; it is not a redistributable production voice bundle. Model-card labels alone do not clear all voice dependencies. The telecalling plan documents concrete asset and carrier gates rather than claiming zero licensing risk.

Insurance claims and validated real credit/churn models are outside this lender prototype. No production underwriting decision, financial change or carrier call has been performed. Cloud publication is a scoped synthetic hackathon demo, with actual resources metered by Snowflake.
